# The Overton Window of AI Adoption
## How Disruptive Technologies Become Mainstream

<!-- Speaker notes:
The Overton Window concept, originally from political science, can be adapted to understand how disruptive technologies like AI move from "unthinkable" to "sensible" over time. This framework helps us understand the current AI adoption strategies.
-->

---

# From Unthinkable to Sensible

## Technology Acceptance Stages

- **Unthinkable** → Novel technology seems impossible or threatening
- **Radical** → Early adopters experiment, mainstream resists  
- **Acceptable** → External pressures create openings
- **Sensible** → Technology becomes normalized and expected

**Key Insight**: "People buy what they know and understand"

<!-- Speaker notes:
This progression mirrors historical disruptions like the Luddite movement where textile workers resisted automated machinery. Today's AI integration follows the same pattern - vendors are positioning AI as IDE assistants rather than replacement tools to make adoption feel less threatening.
-->

---

# AI's Strategic Positioning

## Making the Radical Acceptable

- **Current Strategy**: AI as IDE assistant, not replacement
- **Historical Parallel**: Luddites vs. automated textile machinery
- **Psychological Approach**: Incremental integration into familiar workflows

**The Pattern**: External triggers (economic shifts, competitive pressure) accelerate acceptance

<!-- Speaker notes:
AI vendors are deliberately framing their technology within existing paradigms - code completion, debugging assistance, documentation generation - rather than promoting radical workflow changes. This familiar packaging reduces resistance and moves AI through the acceptance window faster.
-->
